Making things is how we think with our hands. An idea stays weightless until it
is drafted, built, or shipped; only then does it push back and tell you what it
actually wanted to be.

## Prototypes over promises

A rough prototype teaches more in an afternoon than a month of planning. Build
the smallest honest version, put it in front of reality, and listen. The work
will correct you faster and more kindly than any critic.

## Finish, then refine

Finishing is a skill of its own. The last ten percent — the edges, the naming,
the quiet details no one is asked to notice — is where ordinary work becomes
something people trust. Get to done, then make it good.
